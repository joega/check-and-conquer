# The Warpath — campaign story and cinematic direction

Date: 2026-09-11. Status: proposed original story, ready for implementation with `CAMPAIGN_CUTSCENES_PLAN.md`.

## The promise to the player

You command a small army along the Warpath: an ancient route joining five strongholds that once protected one another. Since the alliance fractured, each guardian has closed their section. Villages below the strongholds have paid for that isolation. You set out to reopen the road.

The guardians settle claims of passage on ritual battleboards. Defeating a guardian earns their oath to reopen the way. The matches retain standard chess rules; the story gives those victories a purpose. Combat remains the game's stylized presentation, and checkmate is surrender of command, not the execution of a king.

The campaign begins as a march of conquest and ends with a choice expressed through the commander's actions: keep the strongholds connected instead of claiming a solitary throne. The player needs no branching dialogue or morality system. Each victory reveals a little more of that purpose.

Tone: solemn fantasy adventure, restrained dialogue, strong silhouettes, and a sense of growing scale. Guardians have understandable reasons for resisting. They are not interchangeable villains. Use the existing opponent names and army models, distinguished by framing, environment, gesture, and title; new character models are unnecessary.

Player-facing identity is **Commander**, independent of chosen chess color. The human king represents the commander in establishing shots, but later gestures may use any surviving human piece. Never change or replace a player's selected side to fit a shot.

## Narrative arc and environmental traits

| Chapter | Environmental character | Guardian's belief | What the player earns |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I · The First Gate | Exposed heights, stone, wind, warm signal fire. | Duty means keeping danger outside. | Passage and the first guardian's trust. |
| II · The Cloud Road | Vast sky, vertiginous space, obelisks, distant resonant tones. | Only those who see every consequence should lead. | A route forward despite uncertainty. |
| III · The Winter Crown | Stillness, cold stone, ice, muted sound, patient pacing. | Survival requires holding on to what remains. | A promise to bring help back. |
| IV · The Ember Trial | Heat, heavy silhouettes, braziers, rhythmic sound, restless light. | Strength gives its owner the right to rule. | Passage earned without adopting the tyrant's creed. |
| V · The Final Grove | Overgrown ruins, old arches, green light, space for quiet. | Every conqueror eventually abandons what they take. | Recognition as keeper of the reopened road. |

These traits affect presentation only. No slippery squares, fire damage, weather penalties, altered movement, special powers, or environment-based AI behavior. Existing increasing campaign difficulty remains the gameplay expression of escalating challenge.

## Shared cinematic language

Target intros at **16–22 seconds**, ordinary victory outros at **10–14 seconds**, defeat at **5–7 seconds**, draw at **4–6 seconds**, and final conquest at **20–25 seconds**. Keep Skip available throughout. Each intro has three or four shots: place, guardian, challenge, player readiness. Each victory has consequence, guardian response, and a visual/copy bridge to the next chapter.

Use two or three short text cues per intro/outro, one at a time. Hold each cue at least three seconds, longer for longer lines, with time to see the acting between cues. Label speakers; use separate styling for narration and dialogue. Text is essential in the initial version, voice is optional future production. Shots must work when audio is muted.

Use simple sound design to give each scene shape: an opening environmental/music beat, a brief space under the challenge, and a restrained release into play. Existing arena music, recorded horn, and supported cues are the initial palette. New wind/bell/ice/forge/bird recordings below are desired accents only if already available or legally acquired and documented. Do not substitute weapon impacts for unrelated environmental sounds or promise audio that has not been delivered.

Avoid constant camera movement. Hold on the guardian for the important line. Use foreground geometry and medium shots to create depth within current arena limits. The panorama provides distant scale, not geometry the camera can fly through. No lip-sync requirement: a calm stance and a well-timed supported gesture are preferable to unrelated talking motion.

## Chapter I — Mountain Fortress Terrace

**Story:** The Gatekeeper closed the first gate after the lowland alliance failed to send reinforcements. The gate protects those inside, but it also cuts off those below. The Commander must prove that reopening it will be an act of responsibility.

**Intro, approximately 18 seconds**

1. 0–5 s: reveal the terrace behind a watchtower or brazier, with the mountain panorama beyond. Narration: “For years, the First Gate has opened for no one.”
2. 5–11 s: hold a medium shot on the opposing king, framed by fortress stone. Gatekeeper: “I kept the danger out. Tell me—who will protect the road?”
3. 11–15 s: show the Commander's formation, with one restrained readiness gesture. Commander: “We will. Open the gate.”
4. 15–18 s: return toward the human-side board composition. Objective: “Defeat the Gatekeeper. Reopen the First Gate.”

**Victory, approximately 12 seconds**

The Gatekeeper lowers their stance; the winning formation holds rather than cheering in unison. Gatekeeper: “Then carry our watch beyond these walls.” A warm landmark shot follows. Narration: “The first oath is kept. Above the peaks, the Cloud Road waits.”

Convey reopening through dialogue and a restrained existing fire/light accent; do not require an animated fortress gate absent from the arena.

**Defeat line:** Gatekeeper: “The road asks more of you. Return when you are ready.”

**Map consequence:** First Gate secured; Arcane Sky Citadel becomes the current objective. Retain campaign progress semantics.

## Chapter II — Arcane Sky Citadel

**Story:** The Sky Seer watches possible futures from the citadel and has seen the Warpath destroyed in many of them. Fear of making the wrong choice has become refusal to make any choice. The Commander challenges that paralysis.

**Intro, approximately 20 seconds**

1. 0–5 s: an obelisk fills the foreground, then reveals the sky and citadel. Narration: “Above the storms, the Sky Seer measures every possible ending.”
2. 5–11 s: static guardian shot against open sky. Sky Seer: “I have seen your road burn in a hundred futures.”
3. 11–16 s: hold the Commander among the formation. Commander: “Then help me find the one where it does not.”
4. 16–20 s: descend gently toward board composition. Objective: “Defeat the Sky Seer. Claim passage across the clouds.”

**Victory, approximately 12 seconds**

A supported guardian acknowledgement, followed by a slow obelisk/sky composition. Sky Seer: “One path remains unseen. Perhaps that is where hope lives.” Narration: “Beyond the clouds, the Winter Crown holds its last watch.”

The visual accent is a small, brief obelisk light change if supported. Do not create a full magical bridge or imply the player must navigate one.

**Defeat line:** Sky Seer: “An ending is only one possibility. Choose again.”

**Map consequence:** Cloud Road secured; Frozen Keep becomes current.

## Chapter III — Frozen Keep

**Story:** The Winter Warden has held the keep through years of dwindling supplies. They fear promises because earlier allies never returned. This chapter is the campaign's emotional center: the Commander learns that opening a road creates a duty to travel it both ways.

**Intro, approximately 21 seconds**

1. 0–6 s: still frame through ice spires; let the restrained music establish the cold. Narration: “Winter sealed the keep. The silence of its allies sealed its heart.”
2. 6–12 s: hold the Warden against ice and stone. Winter Warden: “Others promised to return. We buried those promises in the snow.”
3. 12–17 s: close enough to read the Commander's stance without exposing face-rig limits. Commander: “Then judge me by the road I leave open.”
4. 17–21 s: ease out to the board. Objective: “Defeat the Winter Warden. Restore the northern passage.”

**Victory, approximately 13 seconds**

Give the defeated side a quiet pause before the winning gesture. Winter Warden: “When the thaw comes, let it find us with allies.” Narration: “The northern oath is renewed. Beneath the mountains, the forge still burns.”

Keep the environment frozen. Do not instantly melt the keep or imply that winning a match reverses its climate. A warmer composition on the human formation is enough.

**Defeat line:** Winter Warden: “Endure. The road will still be here.”

**Map consequence:** Northern passage secured; Lava Forge becomes current.

## Chapter IV — Lava Forge

**Story:** The Forge Tyrant supplied the old alliance and believes the strongholds owe obedience in return. They respect force and see the Commander's reopened road as an empire in the making. This chapter tests what victory means to the Commander.

**Intro, approximately 18 seconds**

1. 0–5 s: low but safe shot past a real brazier; forge panorama establishes scale. Narration: “At the heart of the mountain, strength has become a throne.”
2. 5–10 s: guardian framed against warm light; supported weapon-ready gesture, no custom hammer act required. Forge Tyrant: “You take gates and crowns. Why pretend you are different?”
3. 10–15 s: hold the Commander in neutral readable light. Commander: “Because the road will belong to everyone.”
4. 15–18 s: settle to board. Objective: “Defeat the Forge Tyrant. Break their claim on the Warpath.”

**Victory, approximately 12 seconds**

The opposing formation becomes still while the human king returns to a composed stance. Forge Tyrant: “You have the strength to rule. We shall see what you do with it.” Narration: “One oath remains, where the first alliance took root.”

Emphasize existing brazier light and the music pulse. Do not destroy the forge, introduce a boss fight, or stage a bespoke execution.

**Defeat line:** Forge Tyrant: “Good intentions cannot hold a line.”

**Map consequence:** Ember Trial complete; Forest Ruins becomes current.

## Chapter V — Forest Ruins

**Story:** The Grove Sovereign guards the ruins where the five strongholds first swore to stand together. They have watched rulers gather titles and then neglect the people those titles were meant to protect. This final match asks whether the Commander can unite without repeating that failure.

**Intro, approximately 22 seconds**

1. 0–6 s: reveal the board through a ruined arch against the grove panorama. Narration: “Here, five strongholds once swore that none would stand alone.”
2. 6–12 s: still guardian shot framed by old stone. Grove Sovereign: “Every conqueror reaches this grove. Few remember why they began.”
3. 12–17 s: Commander and surviving-at-start formation. Commander: “I came to open a road. I intend to keep it open.”
4. 17–22 s: balanced view of both sides, then human-side board composition. Objective: “Defeat the Grove Sovereign. Renew the oath of the Warpath.”

**Final conquest, approximately 24 seconds**

1. 0–6 s: after final combat has completely settled, hold both kings in frame where possible, otherwise cut between their current positions. Grove Sovereign: “Then take no throne. Take the watch.”
2. 6–12 s: the Commander gives one restrained acknowledgement; nearby surviving allies respond in a small staggered sequence. Commander: “No stronghold stands alone again.”
3. 12–19 s: pull back through a verified grove composition. Narration: “Five oaths renewed. One road reopened.”
4. 19–24 s: hold the wide shot beneath “WARPATH CONQUERED” and “The road is open. Your watch begins.” Let the musical phrase resolve, restore the board, and show the result panel.

The result-panel map action leads to the existing completed campaign map. Its completion text should echo the story: “Five strongholds. One open road.” Preserve practice/replay options. Do not automatically leave the result screen or erase access to PGN review.

No kneeling, ceremonial item transfer, new throne, populated village, or traveling montage is required. The ending must be achievable with current actors, geometry, text, light, and camera staging.

**Ordinary repeat victory:** Grove Sovereign: “An oath is kept by returning.” Show an ordinary brief victory, without presenting a second newly completed campaign.

**Defeat line:** Grove Sovereign: “Remember what brought you here. Then return.”

## Shared draw, defeat, surrender, and retry treatment

Draw: a composed view of both armies. Narration: “Neither line yields. The road remains contested.” Follow with the actual domain result, such as stalemate or repetition, in the result panel. No invented winner or unlocked route.

Defeat: pair the chapter's defeat line with a short guardian/losing-formation composition. End on the retry/result panel. Avoid prolonged humiliation, king death, or a second full intro when Restart is chosen.

Surrender: retain the existing army fall presentation and return flow for now. Add no extra defeat movie. Story copy can say “The army withdraws. The road remains contested.” This is an optional text adjustment, not a new domain resignation/PGN feature.

On retry, the persistent chapter/opponent/objective identifies the stakes. The cinematic toggle and Skip always take precedence over narrative pacing. No story event can delay saving an earned unlock.

## Content implementation contract

Keep story text in presentation resources, not GameScreen conditionals. Per-arena definitions should reference intro/victory/defeat text cues plus shared draw content and an optional final-conquest sequence. A cue contains stable ID, speaker label (or narration), text, and start/end timing. Do not duplicate chapter/opponent identity strings already owned by ArenaCatalog; reference or centralize them.

Sequences select human/opponent/winner by bindings rather than fixed board coordinates or colors. Story text is authored above; routine trims for reading time are allowed if meaning remains intact and changes are documented. Avoid adding new lore, characters, quests, inventories, or branching story requirements during implementation.

Use supported semantic gestures after inspecting Animation Browser. Missing acting should fall back to quiet presence, not a random combat attack. Standardize any new semantic alias through the existing animation pipeline. No new asset acquisition is required to deliver this story.

## Narrative acceptance

- Every chapter clearly communicates where the player is, why the guardian resists, and what victory changes.
- The five guardians have distinguishable motives; each outro leads naturally into the next arena.
- Chapter III establishes responsibility, Chapter IV challenges it, and Chapter V resolves it.
- All essential dialogue is readable without sound, never obscured by the chapter card or result panel, and not cut off by natural timeline completion.
- Environmental differences are visible in framing and atmosphere; no chess-rule change is implied in UI or behavior.
- Final conquest is visibly longer and more conclusive than ordinary victory, while remaining skippable and preserving result/review controls.
- Rendered review covers both human colors, sparse end positions, all five chapter transitions, defeat/draw, and final completion. A human can follow the story from captured cinematics without reading this document.

This is original project-authored narrative. New third-party visuals/audio, if acquired later, still require source/license records under the repository's asset rules.
